# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers are the primary readers. They are reviewing Edgar Bonilla G. for UI/UX roles or project work and need to understand fit quickly: domains, judgment, role clarity, availability, and the quality of the shipped interface.

Secondary readers include design leads, recruiters, and collaborators who want evidence of systems thinking, accessibility practice, and product judgment without sorting through vague portfolio language.

## Product Purpose

`(ed)studio` is a personal UI/UX portfolio that proves Edgar's design judgment through accessible product work, visual-system craft, and honest launch evidence. Success means a hiring reader can quickly answer three questions:

- What kind of work does Edgar do?
- How does he think through interface problems?
- How do I contact him with the right context?

## Brand Personality

Clear, rigorous, calm, and specific. The voice should feel evidence-led and human, with plain language over slogans. It should make complex product work easy to inspect without sounding inflated.

## Anti-references

- Generic agency language that could fit any portfolio.
- Invented metrics, unsupported outcomes, or exaggerated claims.
- Dead links, vague calls to action, and unfinished surfaces presented as complete.
- Abstract innovation language from the common buzzword family.
- Repeated slogan cadence that turns every section into a punchline.

## Design Principles

- Lead with fit: show domain, role, availability, and language ability early.
- Make the work inspectable: name the user, constraint, design decision, and current status.
- Keep honesty visible: unfinished items should be labeled clearly, never hidden behind polish.
- Prove accessibility in the writing: use plain labels, standalone links, and state text that does not rely on color.
- Let the portfolio demonstrate the system: copy, tokens, motion, and interaction should all point to the same design discipline.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The portfolio targets WCAG 2.2 AA. UX writing should use plain language, visible labels, accessible names for controls, standalone link text, and status wording that remains clear without color. The default reader may be scanning quickly, using keyboard navigation, zooming, or relying on assistive technology.
